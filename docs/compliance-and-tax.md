# Compliance and tax

Not legal advice. Every figure below is sourced in [sources.md](sources.md); confirm current rates and thresholds with your OMC field officer, CA and Legal Metrology office — slabs, limits and due dates change.

## 1. MDG — the rulebook you are actually inspected against

The **Marketing Discipline Guidelines (MDG)** are issued by the OMCs (IOCL publishes the current consolidated version; HPCL/BPCL issue equivalents). Dealership agreements incorporate them, so they are contractual obligations with a published penalty schedule, not just good practice.

Key obligations relevant to accounting:

| Area | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Legal Metrology | Dispensers correctly stamped and verified; **W&M-verified 5-litre measure** kept at the outlet; seals intact; zero shown to the customer before delivery |
| Daily nozzle test | Dealer tests **every nozzle each day** using the 5-litre measure and records the result |
| Density | Density checked daily before sales, converted to 15 °C and recorded in the density register (Annexure 1 format); records form part of the daily stock register |
| Water dip | Recorded daily, with action taken |
| Receipts | Seals/invoice checked, before/after dips, samples drawn and retained, short delivery documented |
| Stock variation | Reconciled; variation beyond permissible limits after allowing normal operational and evaporation loss is an irregularity (§5.1.11) |
| Records | DSR, density register, complaint register, test records maintained and available for inspection |
| Display | Authorised selling price, complaint box, MDG posters, stock display |

**Penalty shape (MDG §8):** the schedule escalates. Minor, non-critical irregularities with seals intact typically attract a warning-cum-guidance letter first, then minimum amounts in the ₹10,000 range per irregularity for a repeat, rising further from the third occurrence. Critical irregularities (tampered seals, adulteration, short delivery) are handled far more seriously. Under-delivery beyond the Legal Metrology limit stops sales through that unit or nozzle immediately.

**Consequence for your registers:** an incomplete register is itself an irregularity. Keeping them complete and same-day-signed is cheaper than any argument about the product.

## 2. Other licences and inspections

- **PESO** licence for petroleum storage (Class A/B as applicable), with layout and safety compliance.
- **Fire safety** NOC/certificate; fire-fighting equipment maintenance records.
- **Legal Metrology** periodic verification/stamping of dispensing units and measures.
- **State excise / VAT registration**, trade licence, shops & establishments registration.
- **FSSAI** if the outlet sells food/beverages; **PUC** (pollution certificate) facility is expected at outlets.
- **Insurance**: stock, fire, public liability, and employee cover.
- **Weights & Measures sampling** and OMC quality checks — retain samples and reports as per the guideline.

## 3. GST and VAT

- **Petrol (MS) and diesel (HSD) are outside GST** — Section 9(2) of the CGST Act keeps crude, petrol, diesel, natural gas and ATF out of the GST net; they are taxed through **central excise + state VAT**.
- **Lubricants, coolants, accessories, shop goods and services** (car wash, etc.) are **GST-bearing** and need proper tax invoices with the correct CGST/SGST/IGST split and HSN codes.
- Fuel sales still count toward **aggregate turnover**, so any pump selling taxable goods will normally cross the GST registration threshold and must file regular returns.
- Practical bookkeeping consequence: **two sales registers** (VAT for fuel, GST for the rest) and two returns, plus a monthly reconciliation that both match the DSR totals.

## 4. Income tax and TDS

From dealer-focused tax guidance (AY 2026-27 basis):

- The dealer buys and resells on a **principal-to-principal** basis; the dealer margin is **business income**, not an agency commission. So **Section 194H does not ordinarily apply** — it applies only in genuine agency/operator arrangements. Check Form 26AS to see whether anything was deducted.
- **Section 44AD presumptive is rarely available**: turnover must be ≤ ₹2 crore (₹3 crore where cash receipts ≤ 5% of total), and gross fuel turnover almost always exceeds that. Even if available, a deemed 6%/8% of turnover would exceed the real margin.
- **Tax audit (§44AB)** applies above ₹1 crore turnover (₹10 crore where cash receipts and payments are each ≤5%). Most pumps need one. Proprietor → **ITR-3**; firm/LLP → ITR-5; company → ITR-6.
- **Section 194Q**: if prior-year turnover exceeded ₹10 crore, the dealer deducts TDS at 0.1% on purchases above ₹50 lakh from a seller in the year. (TCS under 206C(1H) was omitted from 1 April 2025, shifting the obligation to the buyer.)
- **Cash limits**: §40A(3) disallows a cash expense above ₹10,000 to one person in a day (subject to Rule 6DD exceptions); §269ST prohibits receiving ₹2 lakh or more in cash from one person in a day or for one transaction — which matters for fleet and bulk customers.
- **Depreciation** under §32 on dispensers, tanks, canopy, buildings, generator — written-down value method, prescribed block rates.

## 5. Dealer economics (why margin discipline matters)

The dealer does not set the retail price: OMC-declared commission per litre (revised periodically, differentiated by location/volume) is built into the price. Reported figures cluster around **₹3–4 per litre on petrol and ₹2–3 per litre on diesel**, with city/volume variation — treat these as indicative only and use your own OMC commission circular.

Because the per-litre margin is thin, the P&L is dominated by:

- litres throughput,
- lube and shop margin (much higher per rupee),
- **stock and evaporation losses** (a 0.3% leak on a large throughput is a serious number),
- short/excess cash,
- card/UPI MDR,
- power, salary, maintenance, and interest on working capital.

Which is exactly why the daily reconciliations in [reconciliation.md](reconciliation.md) matter more than any month-end report.

## 6. Record retention

Keep for the statutory periods applicable to income tax, VAT/GST and companies/LLP law — and note that OMC inspection can reach back several years for stock/sales records. A practical minimum for dealers: **8 years** for sales, stock, purchase, VAT/GST and bank records, with DSR/density registers retained for the full dealership term.
